## Technical standards

All content produced by or for Miraya Intelligenza follows these standards automatically.

### Formatting rules

- No em dashes. Restructure the sentence instead.
- Sentence case for all headings. Only the first word and proper nouns are capitalized.
- Oxford comma always. "X, Y, and Z" -- never "X, Y and Z."
- US English spelling throughout. Use "analyze" not "analyse," "optimize" not "optimise."

### Prohibited filler openers

These phrases are banned as sentence or paragraph openers. If a draft contains any of them, rewrite the sentence to open with the actual claim or observation instead.

- "It is worth noting that..."
- "It is important to note that..."
- "It is important to understand that..."
- "Needless to say..."
- "It goes without saying that..."
- "It should be noted that..."
- "One thing to keep in mind is..."

### Prohibited AI-flagged vocabulary

Do not maintain this list manually. Use the two reference sources below as the primary blocklist. They are updated regularly as AI language patterns evolve.

- **Primary reference:** [JustDone -- Common AI words and phrases to avoid](https://justdone.com/blog/ai/common-ai-words) -- organised by category (transition words, buzzwords, paragraph openers) with replacement alternatives for each flagged term.
- **Secondary reference:** [Embryo -- A list of words AI overuses](https://embryo.com/blog/list-words-ai-overuses/) -- cross-referenced across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Deepseek; verified against GPTZero. Updated March 2025.

In addition to everything on those lists, the following terms are specifically banned for Miraya Intelligenza content because they are statistically flagged and directly contradict the brand's standard of precise, specific language:

| Banned term | Use instead |
|---|---|
| Delve | Examine, look at, review |
| Leverage | Use, apply, draw on |
| Unlock | Enable, open, create access to |
| Empower | Give, allow, equip |
| Robust | Specific descriptor (e.g., "tested across 12 edge cases") |
| Cutting-edge | Specific descriptor (e.g., "released in Q1 2025") |

## Editorial process

All content produced for or published under Miraya Intelligenza follows Google's Technical Writing Guidelines (Parts 1 and 2). This process applies to blog posts, articles, documentation, tutorials, and any site copy. It runs in three mandatory stages.

### Stage 1: Initial audit

Before writing or editing begins, scan the existing content for these violations:

- **Passive voice.** Flag every instance. Rewrite to active voice before proceeding.
- **Filler words and zombie nouns.** Identify nominalizations (e.g., "the implementation of" instead of "implementing") and convert them back to verbs.
- **Inconsistent terminology.** Flag any term used two or more ways across the same document. Pick one term and apply it throughout.
- **Poor heading hierarchy.** Verify that H1 leads to H2, H2 leads to H3, and no level is skipped. Each heading must represent a discrete user question or task -- not a topic label.

### Stage 2: Refactor

Apply the following rules in order when rewriting:

1. Convert all passive voice to active voice. The subject of the sentence performs the action.
2. Shorten long sentences. One idea per sentence. If a sentence runs past 25 words, split it.
3. Shorten long paragraphs. Three to five sentences maximum. If a paragraph covers more than one idea, break it.
4. Ensure each paragraph opens with an answer sentence. State the point first. Support it after.
5. Verify that every heading represents a discrete user question or task -- not a category name.

### Stage 3: Verification

Before publishing, confirm all of the following:

- No passive voice remains.
- No zombie nouns remain (nominalizations converted to verbs).
- Terminology is consistent throughout.
- Heading hierarchy is correct (H1 to H2 to H3, no skipped levels).
- Every paragraph opens with its main point.
- All lists follow the correct format: bulleted for items of equal importance, numbered for sequential steps, tables for comparisons or complex data.
- All prohibited filler openers and banned vocabulary from the Technical Standards section have been removed.

### Stage 4: Humanization instructions

Every piece of AI-assisted content produced under the Miraya Intelligenza brand passes through a humanization stage before publication. 

Core rule is to Preserve without exception. Never change the following, regardless of how AI-generated they appear:

- Proper nouns, brand names, product names
- Acronyms and initialisms
- Technical terms that have a precise meaning in the domain
- Code snippets, equations, or mathematical expressions
- Quoted speech and the attribution of that speech
- All facts, figures, statistics, and cited references
- Dates and version numbers

### Self-editing rules

- Read the draft aloud before submission. Any sentence where you stumble is a sentence that needs rewriting.
- Check for zombie nouns. If a noun ends in -tion, -ment, -ance, -ity, or -ness, ask whether the verb form would be cleaner.
- When reviewing another writer's work, cite the specific guideline violated. Do not say "this is unclear." Say "this sentence is passive -- rewrite so the subject performs the action."

### Document structure rules

- Every document longer than 500 words opens with a one-paragraph summary of what the reader will learn.
- Use a logical flow: simple to complex, or chronological where sequence matters.
- Documents longer than 1,000 words include a table of contents.

### Heading rules

- Headings are descriptive, not decorative.
- Each heading answers a specific question the reader is likely to ask at that point.
- Hierarchy is strict: H1 for document title, H2 for major sections, H3 for subsections within those sections.
- Sentence case applies to all headings (see Technical Standards).

### Break the pattern

- AI text runs sentences at uniform length - that uniformity is a detection signal. Vary sentence length.
- AI text produces paragraphs of similar length and structure. Break up uniform paragraphs. 

### Important
- Rewrite the text so it reads as if a skilled human wrote it. Preserve every fact, figure, tone, intent, technical detail, and formatting element from the original. 
- Do not add new information. Do not remove existing information. Output only the humanized text
